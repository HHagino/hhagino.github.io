Independent researcher — operator algebras, measure theory

I work on the boundary between measure-theoretic probability and operator algebras: filtrations of σ-algebras read as towers of abelian von Neumann algebras, and conditional expectations read as averaging operators. The question I keep returning to is when an act of coarse-graining is reversible in structure — when a conditional expectation is genuinely the average of a group of symmetries, and what obstruction appears when it is not.

I am unaffiliated, and I work in the open: preprints carry a DOI from the day they are finished, and the load-bearing lemmas are machine-checked where I can manage it.

Papers

When is a Conditional Expectation a Galois Norm?
A Homogeneity Criterion for Filtration Towers

Preprint, 27 pp. · MSC 46L53, 60A10

For an atomic filtration, the trace-preserving conditional expectation onto an abelian von Neumann subalgebra coincides with the fixed-point average (1/|G|) Σg g of a finite group of trace-preserving automorphisms if and only if the step is homogeneous — the conditional measure is uniform on every fibre. For rooted towers, homogeneity together with spherical homogeneity characterizes exactly those towers realized as the fixed-point tower of a single profinite group. The failure of homogeneity is quantitative: it is an entropy deficit. Under Stone duality the obstruction acquires a categorical home — the conditional kernel is functorial only along strict morphisms — and a closed-form parametric witness is computed in a two-state model.
