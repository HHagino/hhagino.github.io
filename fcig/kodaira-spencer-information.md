## 0. Main result and the correction it forces

Let

\[
\pi:\mathcal X\to B
\]

be a smooth holomorphic family of compact Riemann surfaces of genus \(g\ge2\), equipped fiberwise with the hyperbolic Kähler--Einstein metric. For

\[
E_q:=\pi_*K_{\mathcal X/B}^{q},\qquad q\ge2,
\]

write

\[
m:=q-1,
\]

so that

\[
E_q
=
\pi_*(K_{\mathcal X/B}\otimes K_{\mathcal X/B}^{m})
\]

is in the adjoint high-power form used by Berndtsson and Wan--Zhang [Bern09; WZ21].

For \(\xi\in T_bB\), let \(\mu_\xi\) be the harmonic Kodaira--Spencer representative on \(X_b\). For any \(L^2\)-orthonormal basis \(u_1,\ldots,u_{N_q}\) of \(H^0(X_b,K_{X_b}^q)\), define

\[
\boxed{
\mathfrak K_q(\xi,\bar\eta)
:=
m\sum_{a=1}^{N_q}
\left\langle
(m+\Delta')^{-1}i_{\mu_\xi}u_a,
 i_{\mu_\eta}u_a
\right\rangle .
}
\tag{0.1}
\]

This is exactly the trace of the Kodaira--Spencer resolvent summand of the Berndtsson direct-image curvature formula. It is an intrinsic positive-semidefinite Hermitian tensor on the base.

Define the Weil--Petersson Hermitian pairing **for this note** by

\[
\boxed{
G_{\mathrm{WP}}(\xi,\bar\eta)
:=
\int_{X_b}
\langle\mu_\xi,\mu_\eta\rangle\,\omega_b.
}
\tag{0.2}
\]

Then the curve specialization of the Wan--Zhang trace expansion gives

\[
\boxed{
\mathfrak K_q(\xi,\bar\eta)
=
\frac{q-1}{4\pi}
G_{\mathrm{WP}}(\xi,\bar\eta)
+O(1),
\qquad q\to\infty.
}
\tag{0.3}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\frac{4\pi}{q-1}\mathfrak K_q
\longrightarrow
G_{\mathrm{WP}}.
}
\tag{0.4}
\]

The constant is not fitted. It is forced by

\[
\boxed{
\underbrace{\frac12}_{\text{KS resolvent contribution}}
\times
\underbrace{\frac{q-1}{2\pi}}_{\text{leading curve Bergman density}}
=
\frac{q-1}{4\pi}.
}
\tag{0.5}
\]

At the same time, a second calculation gives a no-go theorem. A classical Fisher metric of a point process whose sample fiber moves with \(b\) depends on the chosen identification of nearby fibers. Under an infinitesimal change of transport generated by \(V_\xi\),

\[
\boxed{
S_\xi^{\tau'}
=
S_\xi^\tau
+
\operatorname{div}_{P}V_\xi.
}
\tag{0.6}
\]

Thus an arbitrary moving-fiber classical Fisher metric is not a canonical tensor on moduli space.

The original FCIG question therefore splits cleanly:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{c}
\text{intrinsic KS/direct-image response}\to G_{\mathrm{WP}}
\quad\text{is controlled},\\[1mm]
\text{classical moving-fiber Fisher}\to G_{\mathrm{WP}}
\quad\text{still requires a transport prescription}.
\end{array}}
\tag{0.7}
\]

# Part I. Curvature-side geometry

## 1. Adjoint high powers and the Kodaira--Spencer field

Put

\[
L:=K_{\mathcal X/B},
\qquad
m=q-1.
\]

Then

\[
E^m:=\pi_*(L^m\otimes K_{\mathcal X/B})=E_q.
\tag{1.1}
\]

This shift is important: the hyperbolic FCIG model uses \(H^0(K^q)\), whereas the direct-image curvature literature naturally writes \(H^0(K\otimes L^m)\).

For the Hermitian weight \(\phi\) on \(L\), the associated horizontal lift has vertical \(\bar\partial\)-derivative

\[
\mu_\xi\in A^{0,1}(X_b,T^{1,0}X_b),
\]

representing the Kodaira--Spencer class of \(\xi\). In the hyperbolic/Teichmüller gauge, take its harmonic representative. The Weil--Petersson metric is its \(L^2\) pairing [Wol86; Sch12].

---

## 2. Berndtsson's exact two-channel curvature formula

**Established.** For the \(L^2\) metric on \(E^m\), the Berndtsson formula used by Wan--Zhang reads, in a base direction \((\xi,\bar\eta)\),

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\langle i\Theta^{E^m}u,u\rangle(\xi,\bar\eta)
&=
\int_{X_b}
m\,c(\phi)(\xi,\bar\eta)
|u|^2e^{-m\phi}
\\
&\quad+
m\left\langle
(m+\Delta')^{-1}i_{\mu_\xi}u,
 i_{\mu_\eta}u
\right\rangle .
\end{aligned}}
\tag{2.1}
\]

Here \(c(\phi)\) is geodesic curvature of the Hermitian weight and \(\Delta'\) is the relevant \((1,0)\)-Laplacian. Berndtsson's theorem gives the positivity framework [Bern09], while Wan--Zhang use (2.1) as the starting point of their high-power expansion [WZ21].

The formula separates two typed mechanisms:

\[
\boxed{
\text{direct-image curvature}
=
\text{horizontal metric/geodesic response}
+
\text{Kodaira--Spencer spectral response}.
}
\tag{2.2}
\]

---

## 3. The intrinsic KS response

### Definition 3.1

Define \(\mathfrak K_q\) by (0.1).

### Proposition 3.2 — basis independence and positivity

\(\mathfrak K_q\) is independent of the orthonormal basis, Hermitian in \((\xi,\eta)\), and positive semidefinite.

### Proof

Let \(T_{\xi\bar\eta}\) be the quadratic-form operator on \(E_q|_b\) determined by

\[
\langle T_{\xi\bar\eta}u,v\rangle
=
m\left\langle
(m+\Delta')^{-1}i_{\mu_\xi}u,
 i_{\mu_\eta}v
\right\rangle.
\]

Then

\[
\mathfrak K_q(\xi,\bar\eta)
=
\operatorname{Tr}_{E_q}T_{\xi\bar\eta},
\]

so basis independence follows from trace invariance. Self-adjointness of \((m+\Delta')^{-1}\) gives Hermitian symmetry. On the diagonal,

\[
\mathfrak K_q(\xi,\bar\xi)
=
m\sum_a
\left\|(m+\Delta')^{-1/2}i_{\mu_\xi}u_a\right\|^2
\ge0.
\]

\(\square\)

**Derived here.** This is operator bookkeeping applied to the established second summand of (2.1).

# Part II. Computing the high-power coefficient

## 4. What the resolvent expansion actually says

Wan--Zhang's Lemma 3.1 gives an exact algebraic expansion of \((m+\Delta')^{-1}\) whose first scalar term is

\[
\frac{1}{2m},
\]

followed by terms involving \(m-\Delta'\), the curvature action \(R^*\), and further resolvents [WZ21].

**Normalization warning.** We do **not** assert the global operator-norm identity

\[
(m+\Delta')^{-1}=\frac1{2m}I+O(m^{-2})
\]

on the entire form space. What is established and used below is Wan--Zhang's resulting asymptotic for the **Kodaira--Spencer matrix elements after the trace/Bergman calculation**.

Their trace computation has the factorized form

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\frac{1}{2\pi}\operatorname{Tr}\Theta^{E^m}(\xi,\bar\xi)
&=
\frac{1}{2\pi}
\int_{X_b}
\left[
-i m c(\phi)(\xi,\bar\xi)
+rac12|\mu_\xi|^2
+O(m^{-1})
\right]
\\
&\qquad\qquad\times
\left[
m-\frac{\rho}{2}+O(m^{-1})
\right]
\frac{\omega_b}{2\pi}
\end{aligned}}
\tag{4.1}
\]

in complex fiber dimension one, with the \(O(m^{-1})\) terms explicitly resolved further in [WZ21].

The **isolated KS trace channel** is therefore

\[
\boxed{
\mathfrak K_q(\xi,\bar\xi)
=
\int_{X_b}
\left[
\frac12|\mu_\xi|^2+O(m^{-1})
\right]
\left[
m-\frac{\rho}{2}+O(m^{-1})
\right]
\frac{\omega_b}{2\pi}.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Equation (4.2), rather than a global resolvent operator approximation, is the precise input to the FCIG deduction.

**FCIG slogan.**

\[
\boxed{
\text{the KS channel pays a spectral factor }\frac12,
\text{ then the Bergman trace counts }\sim\frac{m}{2\pi}\text{ states per area}.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

## 5. Hyperbolic Bergman normalization

In the Wan--Zhang convention, the curve Bergman expansion is

\[
B_q(x)
=
\frac1{2\pi}
\left(m-\frac\rho2\right)
+O(m^{-1}).
\tag{5.1}
\]

For the hyperbolic Kähler--Einstein metric, \(\rho=-1\), so

\[
\boxed{
B_q(x)
=
\frac{m}{2\pi}
+
\frac{1}{4\pi}
+O(m^{-1}).
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

This matches the exact local factor in [`hyperbolic-model.md`](hyperbolic-model.md):

\[
\frac{2q-1}{4\pi}
=
\frac{m}{2\pi}+rac{1}{4\pi}.
\tag{5.3}
\]

The exact global geodesic-loop sector in the hyperbolic model is exponentially small in \(q\) on a fixed thick compact family, so it cannot alter the coefficient of \(m\).

## 6. Theorem 6.1 — Kodaira--Spencer/Weil--Petersson limit

For a smooth family of compact hyperbolic Riemann surfaces, and for fixed base directions \(\xi,\eta\),

\[
\boxed{
\mathfrak K_q(\xi,\bar\eta)
=
\frac{q-1}{4\pi}
G_{\mathrm{WP}}(\xi,\bar\eta)
+O(1),
\qquad q\to\infty.
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

Consequently,

\[
\boxed{
\lim_{q\to\infty}
\frac{4\pi}{q-1}
\mathfrak K_q(\xi,\bar\eta)
=
G_{\mathrm{WP}}(\xi,\bar\eta).
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

The remainder is pointwise in the smooth family and locally uniform under the geometric bounds required by the high-power expansion; no uniform statement through nodal/Deligne--Mumford degeneration is made.

### Proof

On the diagonal, (4.2) gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathfrak K_q(\xi,\bar\xi)
&=
\int_{X_b}
\left[
\frac12|\mu_\xi|^2+O(m^{-1})
\right]
\left[m+O(1)\right]
\frac{\omega_b}{2\pi}
\\
&=
\frac{m}{4\pi}
\int_{X_b}|\mu_\xi|^2\omega_b
+O(1)
\\
&=
\frac{m}{4\pi}
G_{\mathrm{WP}}(\xi,\bar\xi)+O(1).
\end{aligned}
\]

The off-diagonal Hermitian statement follows by polarization. Since \(m=q-1\), (6.1) follows, and division by \(m/(4\pi)\) gives (6.2). \(\square\)

**Status:** **Derived here from [WZ21] plus the standard \(L^2\) description of the Weil--Petersson metric [Wol86; Sch12].** This label is not a novelty claim; an equivalent theorem may already exist in another formulation.

## 7. Chern-normalized KS channel

Wan--Zhang use the convention

\[
-i\,c_1(E^m,\|\cdot\|_m)
=
\frac{1}{2\pi}\operatorname{Tr}\Theta^{E^m}.
\tag{7.1}
\]

Thus if

\[
\widehat{\mathfrak K}_q
:=
\frac{1}{2\pi}\mathfrak K_q,
\]

then

\[
\boxed{
\widehat{\mathfrak K}_q
=
\frac{q-1}{8\pi^2}G_{\mathrm{WP}}+O(1).
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

This is the Chern-normalized contribution of the isolated KS channel in the Wan--Zhang convention.

### Normalization firewall

The earlier FCIG hyperbolic note quotes the Zograf--Takhtajan result

\[
c_1(\lambda_q,h_Q)
=
\frac{6q^2-6q+1}{12\pi^2}\omega_{\mathrm{WP}}
\tag{7.3}
\]

in the convention used there [ZT87]. Equation (7.2) uses the explicitly defined Hermitian pairing \(G_{\mathrm{WP}}\) from (0.2), whereas (7.3) is a \((1,1)\)-form statement. The literature differs in placement of \(i\), \(1/2\), and \(2\pi\).

Therefore no numerical coefficient is cross-identified between (7.2) and (7.3) until a separate convention crosswalk is completed.

# Part III. Quillen placement

## 8. The torsion scale on curve fibers

Wan--Zhang compare the \(L^2\) direct-image curvature and the Quillen curvature and prove

\[
\partial\bar\partial\log\tau_m(\bar\partial)^2
=o(m^{n-1}).
\tag{8.1}
\]

For complex curve fibers \(n=1\),

\[
\boxed{
\partial\bar\partial\log\tau_m(\bar\partial)^2=o(1).
}
\tag{8.2}
\]

They also record in Remark 1.5 that the compact Teichmüller canonical case has stronger exponential behavior [WZ21].

Hence the \(O(m)\) coefficient found in the direct-image KS response is not produced or cancelled by an \(O(m)\) analytic-torsion curvature term.

This does **not** turn the isolated KS summand into a standalone Quillen curvature. It says only that the total \(L^2\)-to-Quillen correction is too small to alter this high-power coefficient.

# Part IV. Statistical transport and the Fisher no-go

## 9. Why a moving sample space is different

For a fixed sample space, Fisher information is invariant under parameter-independent changes of sample coordinates. In a family \(X_b\), however, the sample space itself moves.

For the \(N_q\)-point Bergman process, use the regular configuration locus

\[
Y_b^\circ
=
\left(X_b^{N_q}\setminus\mathcal Z_b\right)/S_{N_q},
\]

where \(\mathcal Z_b\) contains the collision/zero set on which the determinantal density vanishes. To differentiate the probability law with respect to \(b\), choose a local transport

\[
\tau_b:Y_{b_0}^\circ\to Y_b^\circ,
\qquad
\tau_{b_0}=\mathrm{id},
\tag{9.1}
\]

and pull all measures back to \(Y_{b_0}^\circ\).

Write the pulled-back density as \(P_b^\tau\) and its score as

\[
S_\xi^\tau
:=
\left.D_\xi\log P_b^\tau\right|_{b_0}.
\tag{9.2}
\]

---

## 10. Theorem 10.1 — transport-gauge transformation law

Let \(\tau\) and \(\tau'\) be two local transports related by

\[
\tau'_b=\tau_b\circ F_b,
\qquad
F_{b_0}=\mathrm{id}.
\]

Let \(V_\xi\) be the infinitesimal generator of \(F_b\) in direction \(\xi\). Define probability divergence by

\[
\mathcal L_{V_\xi}P_0
=
(\operatorname{div}_{P_0}V_\xi)P_0.
\tag{10.1}
\]

Then, with this pullback convention,

\[
\boxed{
S_\xi^{\tau'}
=
S_\xi^\tau
+
\operatorname{div}_{P_0}V_\xi.
}
\tag{10.2}
\]

Consequently, with

\[
\delta_\xi:=\operatorname{div}_{P_0}V_\xi,
\]

the Fisher pairing transforms as

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
I^{\tau'}(\xi,\bar\eta)-I^\tau(\xi,\bar\eta)
&=
\mathbb E[S_\xi^\tau\overline{\delta_\eta}]
+
\mathbb E[\delta_\xi\overline{S_\eta^\tau}]
+
\mathbb E[\delta_\xi\overline{\delta_\eta}].
\end{aligned}}
\tag{10.3}
\]

### Proof

Because

\[
P_b^{\tau'}
=(\tau'_b)^*P_b
=F_b^*(\tau_b^*P_b),
\]

differentiation at \(b_0\) yields

\[
D_\xi P_b^{\tau'}
=
D_\xi P_b^\tau
+
\mathcal L_{V_\xi}P_0.
\]

Divide by \(P_0\) to obtain (10.2). Under the usual compact/support assumptions, both a normalized score and a probability divergence have zero expectation. Expanding the \(L^2(P_0)\) pairing of \(S^\tau+\delta\) gives (10.3). \(\square\)

### Corollary 10.2 — transport-gauge no-go

A classical Fisher tensor on a moving family of configuration spaces is not invariant under arbitrary parameter-dependent changes of transport.

In particular, a Teichmüller marking supplies topological control but does not by itself make all smooth representatives of the fiber identification statistically equivalent.

**Derived here / no-go.**

**Slogan:**

\[
\boxed{
\text{change the fiber identification}
\Longrightarrow
\text{add a probability-divergence field to the score}.
}
\tag{10.4}
\]

# Part V. Information Closure v2

## 11. What has actually been closed

Theorem 6.1 gives a controlled intrinsic chain:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Kodaira--Spencer class}
\xrightarrow{\text{Berndtsson resolvent}}
\mathfrak K_q
\xrightarrow{\;4\pi/(q-1)\;}
G_{\mathrm{WP}}.
}
\tag{11.1}
\]

The coefficient is fixed.

Theorem 10.1 blocks the stronger, untyped chain

\[
\text{arbitrary moving-fiber classical Fisher}
\stackrel{?}{=}
C\,G_{\mathrm{WP}}.
\]

The correct architecture is instead

\[
\boxed{

\\[2mm]

\mathfrak K_q
\\[2mm]
&&\downarrow\;4\pi/(q-1)
\\[2mm]

\tag{11.2}
\]

## 12. Relation to DPP information geometry

Hino--Yano show that finite DPP models are curved exponential families and quantify their departure from exponential-family flatness by an embedding-curvature tensor [HY24]. Their theorem is finite/discrete and is **not** used to prove the continuous Bergman formulas here.

It does, however, reinforce the typing lesson already obtained directly in FCIG:

\[
\boxed{
\text{determinantal form}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{globally flat exponential family}.
}
\tag{12.1}
\]

For fixed complex structure, FCIG already found the exact moving-Kähler defect

\[
\mathcal I_q
=
\operatorname{Hess}\log Z_q
+
\mathfrak D_q^{\mathrm{met}}.
\]

For moving complex structure, Theorem 10.1 adds a distinct transport-gauge issue. These are separate defects and should not be conflated.

# 14. Next theorem target

The question “what is the leading Kodaira--Spencer coefficient?” is now answered by (6.1).

The next genuinely statistical calculation is

\[
\boxed{
I_q^{\tau^{\mathrm{KE}}}-\mathfrak K_q,
}
\tag{14.1}
\]

where \(\tau^{\mathrm{KE}}\) is the transport generated locally by the Kähler--Einstein/Chern horizontal lift used to define the geometric family.

There are three logically distinct possible outcomes:

1. **canonical-transport closure:**
   \[
   \frac{4\pi}{q-1}I_q^{\tau^{\mathrm{KE}}}
   \to G_{\mathrm{WP}};
   \]
2. **explicit defect:** the limit differs from \(G_{\mathrm{WP}}\) by a computable probability-divergence/second-fundamental-form tensor;
3. **no canonical classical closure:** replace classical point-process Fisher by a transport quotient or an intrinsic Hilbert/Grassmannian information metric.

No outcome is assumed in advance.
