## 0. Result in one page

The probability-only no-go says that a bare atomless law has too much relabelling symmetry to select nontrivial finite-rank modes. A minimal and standard analytic symmetry-breaking datum is a reversible Markov dynamics, equivalently a symmetric Dirichlet form

\[
\mathcal E:\mathcal F\times\mathcal F\longrightarrow\mathbb C
\quad\text{on }L^2(\Omega,p).
\]

The Dirichlet form canonically supplies a Hilbert module of one-forms \(\mathcal H_1\) and a closed derivation

\[
\partial:\mathcal F\longrightarrow\mathcal H_1
\]

such that

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal E(f,g)=\langle\partial f,\partial g\rangle_{\mathcal H_1},
\qquad A=\partial^*\partial .
}
\tag{0.1}
\]

On the exact sector \(\mathcal H_{\rm ex}:=\overline{\operatorname{ran}\partial}\), define

\[
\boxed{
\mathscr D_{\mathcal E}
=
\begin{pmatrix}0&\partial^*\\ \partial&0\end{pmatrix}
\quad\text{on}\quad L^2(\Omega,p)\oplus\mathcal H_{\rm ex}.
}
\tag{0.2}
\]

Then

\[
\mathscr D_{\mathcal E}^{\,2}
=A\oplus\partial\partial^*.
\tag{0.3}
\]

If the form is irreducible, \(A\) has compact resolvent, and its first positive eigenvalue is separated from \(0\), then

\[
\operatorname{spec}\mathscr D_{\mathcal E}
=\{0\}\cup\{\pm\sqrt{\lambda_n}:n\ge1\},
\tag{0.4}
\]

and the chiral part is Fredholm with

\[
\ker\partial=\mathbb C\mathbf1,\qquad
\ker\partial^*|_{\mathcal H_{\rm ex}}=0,\qquad
\operatorname{ind}\partial=1.
\tag{0.5}
\]

This is a genuine enriched selector

\[
\boxed{(p,\mathcal E)\longmapsto\mathscr D_{\mathcal E}.}
\]

But its scalar determinant line is canonically trivialized by \(\mathbf1\). Nontrivial normalization topology still requires a twisted coefficient module, complex polarization, or phase-sensitive connection.

\[
\boxed{
\text{Markov locality selects a differential operator;}
\quad
\text{twisting selects nontrivial topology.}
}
\]

## 1. Input: reversible statistical dynamics

Let \((\Omega,\mathcal B,p)\) be a standard probability space. A densely defined, closed, nonnegative symmetric form \((\mathcal E,\mathcal F)\) on \(L^2(\Omega,p)\) is a Dirichlet form when it satisfies the Markov contraction property. Assume

\[
\mathbf1\in\mathcal F,\qquad \mathcal E(\mathbf1)=0,
\tag{1.1}
\]

and write \(A\ge0\) for its self-adjoint generator:

\[
\mathcal E(f,g)=\langle A^{1/2}f,A^{1/2}g\rangle,
\qquad P_t=e^{-tA}.
\tag{1.2}
\]

The semigroup \(P_t\) is reversible and \(p\) is stationary, but \(p\) does not determine \(P_t\).

\[
\boxed{
p\text{ says where mass sits;}
\qquad
\mathcal E\text{ says which variations are local and costly.}
}
\tag{1.3}
\]

## 2. First-order calculus

The Cipriani–Sauvageot construction produces a Hilbert module \(\mathcal H_1\) and a closable derivation satisfying, on a core,

\[
\partial(fg)=f\,\partial g+g\,\partial f,
\qquad
\|\partial f\|_{\mathcal H_1}^2=\mathcal E(f).
\tag{2.1}
\]

For a strongly local regular form, the module product is encoded by the energy measure:

\[
\langle f\,\partial g,h\,\partial k\rangle_{\mathcal H_1}
=\int_\Omega \overline f h\,d\Gamma(g,k).
\tag{2.2}
\]

The adjoint \(\partial^*\) is abstract divergence and, with the positive convention,

\[
\boxed{A=\partial^*\partial.}
\tag{2.3}
\]

These constructions are established in [CS03] and developed for regular metric-measure Dirichlet forms in [HRT13].

## 3. Exact Dirichlet–Hodge operator

The full one-form module may contain coexact or harmonic sectors not generated by scalar gradients. To avoid silently adding that topology, restrict to

\[
\mathcal H_{\rm ex}=\overline{\operatorname{ran}\partial}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

### Proposition 3.1

On \(L^2(\Omega,p)\oplus\mathcal H_{\rm ex}\), the natural closed block operator

\[
\mathscr D_{\mathcal E}
\binom f\omega
=\binom{\partial^*\omega}{\partial f}
\tag{3.2}
\]

is self-adjoint, odd, and satisfies

\[
\boxed{\mathscr D_{\mathcal E}^2=A\oplus\partial\partial^*.}
\tag{3.3}
\]

#### Proof

This is the standard self-adjoint block construction associated with a densely defined closed operator and its adjoint. Matrix multiplication on the square domain gives (3.3). \(\square\)

## 4. Spectral theorem

Assume:

1. \(\ker A=\mathbb C\mathbf1\);
2. \((A+1)^{-1}\) is compact;
3. the first positive eigenvalue satisfies \(\lambda_1>0\).

### Theorem 4.1 — spectral square-root selector

For

\[
0=\lambda_0<\lambda_1\le\lambda_2\le\cdots,\qquad\lambda_n\to\infty,
\]

one has

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{spec}\mathscr D_{\mathcal E}
=\{0\}\cup\{\pm\sqrt{\lambda_n}:n\ge1\}.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

Moreover, \(\mathscr D_{\mathcal E}\) has compact resolvent and

\[
\boxed{\ker\mathscr D_{\mathcal E}=\mathbb C\mathbf1\oplus0.}
\tag{4.2}
\]

#### Proof

For an orthonormal eigenbasis \(Ae_n=\lambda_ne_n\), put

\[
\eta_n=\lambda_n^{-1/2}\partial e_n.
\]

Equation (2.1) makes \(\{\eta_n\}\) orthonormal. The spectral gap makes \(\operatorname{ran}\partial\) closed, so these vectors span \(\mathcal H_{\rm ex}\). On the span of \((e_n,0)\) and \((0,\eta_n)\), the operator matrix is

\[
\begin{pmatrix}0&\sqrt{\lambda_n}\\ \sqrt{\lambda_n}&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]

This proves (4.1), and compactness follows from \(\lambda_n\to\infty\). Finally,

\[
\ker\partial^*\cap\overline{\operatorname{ran}\partial}
=(\overline{\operatorname{ran}\partial})^\perp
\cap\overline{\operatorname{ran}\partial}=0,
\]

which proves (4.2). \(\square\)

## 5. Fredholm and determinant consequence

### Corollary 5.1

The chiral part

\[
\mathscr D_{\mathcal E}^+=\partial:
\operatorname{Dom}\partial\subset L^2(\Omega,p)\to\mathcal H_{\rm ex}
\]

is Fredholm and

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{ind}\mathscr D_{\mathcal E}^+=1.}
\tag{5.1}
\]

Its determinant line is

\[
\lambda_{\mathcal E}
=\det(\mathbb C\mathbf1)\otimes\det(0)^{-1}
\cong\mathbb C,
\tag{5.2}
\]

canonically trivialized by the normalized constant function \(\mathbf1\).

### No-go 5.2 — scalar exact topology

\[
\boxed{c_1(\lambda_{\mathcal E})=0.}
\tag{5.3}
\]

Thus adding Markov dynamics resolves operator underdetermination, but not topological underdetermination.

## 6. Naturality

Let \(T:(\Omega,p,\mathcal E)\to(\Omega',p',\mathcal E')\) be a measure-preserving isomorphism satisfying

\[
\mathcal E(U_Tf,U_Tg)=\mathcal E'(f,g).
\tag{6.1}
\]

The universal differential construction induces \(U_T^{(1)}\) with

\[
U_T^{(1)}\partial'=\partial U_T.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
(U_T\oplus U_T^{(1)})\mathscr D_{\mathcal E'}
=\mathscr D_{\mathcal E}(U_T\oplus U_T^{(1)}).
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Sel}_{\rm DH}:\mathsf{DirProb}\to\mathsf{OddOp},
\qquad
(p,\mathcal E)\mapsto\mathscr D_{\mathcal E}
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

is functorial for energy-preserving probability isomorphisms.

## 7. Finite reversible chain

Let \(\Omega=\{1,\dots,m\}\), \(p_i>0\), and let jump rates satisfy detailed balance

\[
p_iq_{ij}=p_jq_{ji}=:c_{ij}.
\tag{7.1}
\]

Then

\[
\mathcal E(f)=\frac12\sum_{i,j}c_{ij}|f_i-f_j|^2.
\tag{7.2}
\]

After choosing one edge orientation, set

\[
(\partial f)_{ij}=\sqrt{c_{ij}}(f_j-f_i).
\tag{7.3}
\]

With compatible vertex and edge inner products, \(A=\partial^*\partial\) is the reversible graph Laplacian and

\[
\mathscr D_{\mathcal E}
=\begin{pmatrix}0&\partial^*\\ \partial&0\end{pmatrix}
\tag{7.4}
\]

is its exact Hodge–Dirac matrix. For a connected graph, \(\ker A=\mathbb C\mathbf1\).

\[
\boxed{
\{p_i\}\text{ does not determine }\{c_{ij}\};
\quad
\{c_{ij}\}\text{ determines locality and spectrum.}
}
\tag{7.5}
\]

## 8. Fisher geometry is a separate direction

For a family \(b\mapsto(p_b,\mathcal E_b)\), there are two geometries:

\[
g^F_b(X,Y)
=\mathbb E_{p_b}[(X\log p_b)(Y\log p_b)]
\tag{8.1}
\]

on parameter space, and the spectral geometry of \(A_b\) on sample space. Dirichlet axioms do not imply

\[
g^F=\partial\bar\partial\log\det{}'A.
\tag{8.2}
\]

That is an extra response identity requiring a coupling between variation of \(p_b\) and variation of the local energy.

\[
\boxed{
\text{sample-space locality from }\mathcal E_b
\neq
\text{parameter-space distinguishability from }g^F_b.
}
\tag{8.3}
\]

## 9. Why complex polarization is still missing

A real Dirichlet calculus does not canonically split

\[
d=\partial_J+\bar\partial_J
\tag{9.1}
\]

because that requires an almost-complex structure on the cotangent module, plus integrability. Nor does it choose a Hermitian coefficient line with nonzero Chern class.

The corrected reconstruction ladder is

\[
\boxed{
p
\xrightarrow{\ +\mathcal E\ }
\mathscr D_{\rm real}
\xrightarrow{\ +J,L,\nabla^L\ }
D_{\bar\partial,L}
\xrightarrow{\ \det\ }
(\lambda,h_Q,\nabla^Q).
}
\tag{9.2}
\]

This is where Čech data re-enter: local coefficient frames and their transition functions cannot be recovered from scalar energy alone.

## 10. Gate ledger

- **DHS-A — PASS:** a reversible Dirichlet form produces one-forms and a closed derivation.
- **DHS-B — PASS:** the exact block operator is self-adjoint and squares to the scalar/exact-form Laplacians.
- **DHS-C — PASS:** compact resolvent, irreducibility and a gap give its explicit spectrum.
- **DHS-D — PASS:** naturality under energy-preserving probability isomorphisms.
- **DHS-E — PASS:** finite reversible chains give exact incidence-matrix realizations.
- **DHS-F — NO-GO:** the scalar exact determinant line is canonically trivial.
- **DHS-G — NO-GO:** Fisher and spectral/Quillen Hessians need not agree.
- **DHS-H — OPEN:** define and integrate a complex structure on the Dirichlet cotangent module.
- **DHS-I — OPEN:** select a coefficient line/local system with nonzero differential Chern data.
- **DHS-J — OPEN:** realize the enriched ladder in the hyperbolic Bergman/BLS model.

\boxed{
\text{Measure gives normalization; dynamics gives calculus; polarization gives holomorphic topology.}
