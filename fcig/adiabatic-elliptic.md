# FCIG Model XX — Adiabatic elliptic family / Kaluza--Klein response

**Status:** Gate CD--CG local sector derived; finite modular threshold remains open. No Einstein equation or horizon law is inferred.

## 1. Goal

Model XIX established the exact moduli-space identity

\[
-\partial\bar\partial\log\det{}'\Delta_\tau=F_{\lambda_H}.
\]

That is a Chern-curvature statement on the space of **constant** elliptic structures. Model XX asks the next typed question: if

\[
\tau=\tau(x)=u(x)+iY(x)
\]

varies over a four-dimensional base, what two-derivative spacetime response is generated by the intrinsic torus operator?

The first pass is deliberately restrictive so every term is explicit.

---

## 2. Fixed local family geometry

Let \((M_4,g_{\mu\nu})\) be a Euclidean base and use a locally trivial torus fibration

\[
X_6=M_4\times T^2.
\]

Freeze the torus area scale \(L^2\), the Kaluza--Klein vectors, and all off-diagonal base--fiber metric components. Take

\[
\boxed{
 ds_6^2
 =g_{\mu\nu}(x)dx^\mu dx^\nu
 +L^2G_{ab}(\tau(x))dy^a dy^b,
}
\]

with \(y^a\sim y^a+1\) and

\[
\boxed{
G(\tau)=\frac1Y
\begin{pmatrix}
1&u\\
u&u^2+Y^2
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad \det G=1.
}
\]

Thus

\[
\sqrt{g_6}=L^2\sqrt{g_4}.
\]

The fixed determinant is essential: the volume modulus is not being mixed into the complex-structure calculation.

Toroidal dimensional reduction and its scalar coset metrics are standard; see Maharana--Schwarz [MaharanaSchwarz1993].

---

## 3. Exact scalar Laplacian and the absence of local mode mixing

For a minimally coupled scalar, the positive six-dimensional Laplacian is

\[
\Delta_6
=-\frac1{\sqrt{g_6}}\partial_M
\left(\sqrt{g_6}\,g_6^{MN}\partial_N\right).
\]

Because the metric is block diagonal, \(G_{ab}\) is independent of the fiber coordinates, and \(\det G=1\), this becomes exactly

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_6
=\Delta_4
-\frac1{L^2}G^{ab}(\tau(x))\partial_a\partial_b.
}
\]

Use the **coordinate** Fourier basis

\[
\chi_{m,n}(y)=e^{2\pi i(my^1+ny^2)},
\qquad (m,n)\in\mathbb Z^2.
\]

This basis is independent of \(x\) and of \(\tau(x)\) in the chosen local trivialization. Expanding

\[
\Psi(x,y)=\sum_{m,n}\phi_{m,n}(x)\chi_{m,n}(y)
\]

gives

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_6\Psi
=\sum_{m,n}
\left[\Delta_4+M_{m,n}^2(\tau(x))\right]\phi_{m,n}\,\chi_{m,n},
}
\]

with

\[
\boxed{
M_{m,n}^2(\tau)
=\frac{4\pi^2}{L^2Y}|m\tau-n|^2.
}
\]

Therefore:

> **Result XX.1 (restricted local no-mixing theorem).** In the block-diagonal, fixed-volume, locally trivial scalar model, the instantaneous KK decomposition has no local Berry connection or off-diagonal mode mixing. All local \(\tau(x)\)-dependence enters through the intrinsic KK masses.

This statement is intentionally local. A nontrivial torus bundle, KK vectors, modular monodromy, or a different field representation can reintroduce connection/mixing data. Globally, modular transformations relabel the integer lattice as in Model XIX.

This corrects the overly generic expectation that a varying modulus must automatically generate a nonzero mode-basis connection.

## 4. Total-space scalar curvature

For the same fixed-volume block metric, direct Christoffel reduction gives

\[
\boxed{
R_6
=R_4
-\frac14 g^{\mu\nu}
\operatorname{Tr}
\left(
G^{-1}\partial_\mu G\,
G^{-1}\partial_\nu G
\right).
}
\]

This is the standard toroidal shape-modulus structure appearing in dimensional reduction [MaharanaSchwarz1993].

For the elliptic matrix above one finds exactly

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Tr}
\left(G^{-1}dG\,G^{-1}dG\right)
=2\frac{du^2+dY^2}{Y^2}.
}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
R_6
=R_4
-\frac1{2Y^2}
\left(
\partial_\mu u\partial^\mu u
+\partial_\mu Y\partial^\mu Y
\right).
}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
R_6
=R_4
-\frac1{2Y^2}\partial_\mu\tau\partial^\mu\bar\tau.
\]

> **Result XX.2.** The Poincare shape of the spacetime two-derivative modulus invariant is already forced by the intrinsic total-space torus geometry; it is not obtained by fitting a mass map.

This is a geometric statement about the six-dimensional metric, not yet a quantum prediction for its coefficient.

## 5. One-loop local UV sector from the six-dimensional heat kernel

For one real minimally coupled scalar,

\[
W_{1\text{-loop}}
=\frac12\log\det\Delta_6
=-\frac12\int_{\epsilon}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{t}
\operatorname{Tr}(e^{-t\Delta_6}).
\]

The standard six-dimensional heat trace contains [Vassilevich2003]

\[
\operatorname{Tr}(e^{-t\Delta_6})
\sim
(4\pi t)^{-3}
\int_{X_6}\sqrt{g_6}
\left(1+t\frac{R_6}{6}+\cdots\right).
\]

In a sharp proper-time convention \(\epsilon=\Lambda^{-2}\), the curvature divergence is

\[
\boxed{
W_{\rm div}^{(R)}
=-\frac{\Lambda^4}{24(4\pi)^3}
\int_{X_6}\sqrt{g_6}\,R_6
}
\]

up to lower-power/finite pieces and the chosen regulator convention.

Using \(\sqrt{g_6}=L^2\sqrt{g_4}\) and Result XX.2 gives the modulus part

\[
\boxed{
W_{\rm div}^{(\tau)}
=\frac{\Lambda^4L^2}{48(4\pi)^3}
\int_{M_4}\sqrt{g_4}\,
\frac{\partial_\mu\tau\partial^\mu\bar\tau}{Y^2}.
}
\]

Thus the **shape** is exactly Poincare, while the coefficient is power divergent and renormalizes a local higher-dimensional Einstein--Hilbert / shape-modulus counterterm.

A different regulator can move power-divergent pieces, but not remove the need for the corresponding allowed local counterterm. The normalization is therefore not a scheme-independent FCIG prediction.

> **Result XX.3 (UV-local separation).** The intrinsic family does generate a genuine spacetime Poincare-shaped kinetic invariant, but its leading local one-loop normalization is UV/counterterm sensitive.

This cleanly differs from Model XIX, where the mixed moduli Chern curvature of the zeta determinant was finite and exact.

Von Gersdorff's torus heat-kernel formalism explicitly separates UV-sensitive local renormalization from UV-insensitive nonlocal contributions in compactified one-loop effective actions [vonGersdorff2008].

## 6. KK-tower view and consistency of the divergence

Result XX.1 lets us use the Model-XVIII bubble response mode by mode in this restricted local model. Excluding the massless zero mode, the formal tower contribution is

\[
W_{(2)}^{\rm KK}
=\frac1{384\pi^2}
\sum_{(m,n)\ne(0,0)}
\int_{M_4}\sqrt{g_4}\,
\frac{
\partial_\mu M_{m,n}^2
\partial^\mu M_{m,n}^2
}{M_{m,n}^2}
+\cdots.
\]

For large lattice momentum, \(M_{m,n}^2\sim |(m,n)|^2/L^2\), while derivatives with respect to the dimensionless complex structure change the mass by an order-one fraction. Therefore each summand scales like \(M^2\), and the two-dimensional lattice sum is power divergent.

With a physical six-dimensional cutoff, the scaling is

\[
G_{\tau\bar\tau}^{\rm KK,local}
\sim \Lambda^4L^2\,Y^{-2},
\]

matching the six-dimensional heat-kernel result structurally.

The term-by-term KK sum is therefore not a finite substitute for the six-dimensional local renormalization problem.

## 7. What remains potentially predictive

The local Poincare-shaped term is not the end of the calculation. After subtracting/fixing the allowed local counterterm, compactification can leave finite nonlocal or threshold information. The torus heat-kernel literature separates precisely these local and nonlocal sectors [vonGersdorff2008].

The open quantity is therefore

\[
\boxed{
G_{\tau\bar\tau}^{\rm finite}(\tau)
}
\]

in the derivative expansion of the fully regulated family determinant.

The decisive questions are:

1. Is the finite two-derivative threshold nonzero?
2. Is it modular invariant/covariant in the correct sense?
3. Is it a constant multiple of \(Y^{-2}\), or does it carry additional modular dependence?
4. Does it relate to the exact Model-XIX curvature
   \(-\partial\bar\partial\log\det{}'\Delta_\tau\), or are these genuinely distinct spectral responses?

No answer to these four questions is assumed here.

## 8. Cusp / validity test

For

\[
Y\to\infty,
\]

the torus degenerates. From

\[
M_{m,n}^2=\frac{4\pi^2}{L^2Y}|m\tau-n|^2,
\]

modes with \(m=0\) obey

\[
M_{0,n}^2=\frac{4\pi^2n^2}{L^2Y}\to0.
\]

Thus an expansion that integrates out the entire nonzero KK tower cannot remain uniformly valid at the cusp.

> **Result XX.4.** The adiabatic heavy-tower description has a controlled domain away from degeneration; the cusp is an explicit falsification/validity boundary rather than a region where the same local EFT may be extrapolated blindly.

## 9. Relation of Models XVII, XIX, and XX

The three Poincare structures are now sharply distinct:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{lll}
\text{Model XVII:}&G_{\rm hyp}&\text{chosen from pre-existing target geometry for a sigma action},\\
\text{Model XIX:}&-\partial\bar\partial\log\det{}'\Delta&=F_{\lambda_H}\text{ on constant-modulus space},\\
\text{Model XX:}&G_{\rm ad}^{\rm local}&\propto \Lambda^4L^2G_{\rm hyp}\text{ in spacetime, UV-sensitive}.
\end{array}}
\]

The equality of their **tensor shape** is now explained by intrinsic torus geometry. Equality of their physical normalizations is not established.
